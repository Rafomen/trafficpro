Social Media Targeting Methods

Combining these methods is what separates a professional launch from "just running ads". I pick the right mix for every niche and goal

Geo-Targeting

Ads are shown to people within the boundaries you choose: a country, city, neighborhood, or a radius around a specific location. Essential when the customer has to show up in person — a salon, a repair shop, a restaurant. For example, a dental clinic shows ads only to residents of neighborhoods within 5 km of the practice and doesn't pay for impressions of people who would never make the trip.

Interest Targeting

Meta and TikTok know what people engage with on social media: what they follow, like, and save. Ads are shown to those whose interests match your product — running shoes are seen by runners, and cooking masterclass ads are seen by people who collect recipes.

Dayparting

You set the exact hours and days your ads run. If leads come in on weekday daytime but there are no managers on duty in the evening, your budget isn't wasted on nighttime clicks nobody will follow up on. Especially important for search advertising, where you pay for every click.

Socio-Demographic Targeting

Basic but crucial filters: gender, age, marital status, income, job title. They cut out clearly irrelevant audiences before launch: children's products go to parents, premium B2B services go to executives, not students. The cleaner the segment — the lower your cost per lead.

Behavioral Targeting

Algorithms analyze behavior: what people have bought, how they respond to promotions, how often they click on ads. Ads are shown to those who statistically are most likely to complete a purchase. Over time, the system learns from your leads, and the cost per lead steadily drops.

Look-alike

The most powerful method if your business already has customers. You give the system your customer list, it finds common traits and matches millions of users similar to your best buyers. The quality of such an audience is nearly the same as the real customers who already pay you.
